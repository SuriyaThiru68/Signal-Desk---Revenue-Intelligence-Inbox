## Analysis

### Internal billing training exposes exception handling that depends on manual judgment

**Confidence:** high · **Analysis version:** analysis-2026-08-22.1 · **Analysis source revision:** 1

#### What happened

An internal billing walkthrough covered subscription and usage invoicing, due-date exceptions, zero-value invoices, failed wallet deductions and the dunning sequence. Operations creates manual invoices and must adjust the next billing date after a frozen account is cleared. A request for back-office access was deferred to the next training session.

#### Why it matters

This is internal enablement, not a customer conversation. It shows that billing exceptions depend on operations knowing when to void, reissue, clear and reset dates. The process risk is human inconsistency, especially when the displayed due date differs from the allowed collection period.

#### Evidence

- **Turn 7** — “The nominal due period is seven days, but we sometimes give the customer until the end of the month.”
  Displayed due date differs from collection practice.
- **Turn 8** — “The displayed due date and the allowed collection period are not always the same thing.”
  The core exception to document.
- **Turn 13** — “If a periodic invoice has no subscription charge due and no extra usage component, its value can be zero. That is not automatically a missing payment.”
  Zero-value invoices are not collection failures.
- **Turn 15** — “Suppose the invoice is INR 6,240 but the wallet contains INR 6,110. The deduction fails and a notification goes out.”
  Concrete dunning example.
- **Turn 17** — “If it still fails, payments and cards can be blocked, and the billing date stays stuck.”
  Frozen-account state.
- **Turn 18** — “Operations also needs to adjust the next billing date manually.”
  Manual recovery step.
- **Turn 20** — “I will request the back-office access separately for the next training session.”
  Training follow-up.
